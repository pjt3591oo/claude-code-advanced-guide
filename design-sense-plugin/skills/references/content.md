# Content, discovery and conversion

Decide what the reader should understand or do, and what evidence earns that action. Derive information order from questions and intent. Distinguish orientation, explanation, proof, comparison and action; do not fill a generic hero/features/testimonials template regardless of content.

Choose a visual direction grounded in subject matter, audience and existing brand. Typography, imagery and layout should communicate that choice consistently. Use actual copy lengths and useful assets; label missing facts instead of inventing testimonials or product claims.

For discovery, test labels, search, filters, result relevance, no results and return position. For commerce, trace variant selection, availability, price, cart edits and checkout boundaries. For reading, test line length, headings, captions, links, long passages and small screens. Visual emphasis should follow the content's purpose and the next useful action.

Preserve equivalent meaning across text, media and assistive output. Captions may need meaningful sound as well as dialogue; visual-only information needs an appropriate alternative. Check timing and placement, and update alternatives when the source changes. Hidden prose should clarify the same interface, not contradict visible labels or duplicate what is already conveyed.

Validate the requested viewports with real content and focus traversal. Avoid motion, sticky regions or large imagery that obstruct reading or the primary action. Distinguish a functional local demonstration from real subscription, payment or submission integration.
