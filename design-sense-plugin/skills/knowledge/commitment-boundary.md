# Commitment boundaries

Put review where the person gains a useful chance to reconsider. Consider the cost of a mistake and how completely it can be reversed.

A view change can apply immediately. A recoverable local removal may fit Undo. External sending or a booking may need a review of target, conditions and outcome before commitment. That review can already be the final screen; adding another generic confirmation contributes little if it reveals nothing new. Keep consent required by the actual task.

A prototype's simulation notice explains what will happen. It is not automatically a request for consent. Place the explanation near the action without inventing a mandatory checkbox. Conversely, a prototype-looking surface connected to real external effects still has real consequences.

Describe what the person would lose or fail to understand if this step disappeared. A concrete answer justifies the review. Otherwise, combine it with the preceding decision. A retained review should present meaningful information and a usable way to change or cancel the choice.

Make correction a short return path. Reopen the relevant answer with its value intact, then return to review. If the change invalidates dependent choices, resolve those dependencies first; preserve unrelated completed work. A summary that cannot be corrected is only a display, not an effective review.
