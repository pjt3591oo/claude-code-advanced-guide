# Text rhythm

A text role includes typeface, size, weight, line height and tracking. Its visible rhythm also depends on line length, script and the loaded font. A compact display title and a wrapped instruction need different treatment; copying one heading's tight tracking into body text can crowd glyphs without improving hierarchy. Start with the typeface's normal spacing and make optical adjustments against actual language and content.

Inspect three distances separately: between glyphs, between lines of one thought, and between distinct text blocks. Large leading cannot substitute for paragraph separation; a fixed tall wrapper can make a short label float away from its value. Conversely, tight line boxes and equal gaps can merge a title, metadata and description into one indistinct block. Give related text a shared rhythm and separate groups by their relationship.

Use a minimum control size for touch while allowing text-driven height for content. Compare one-line, wrapped and missing secondary text in the same component. Preserve baseline relationships and action alignment as content grows; do not repair one fixture by crushing tracking or clipping the next line. Text enlargement and spacing overrides should preserve content and operations.

Small metadata still has to be read. If fitting it requires extreme reduction, reconsider content or layout before shrinking it again. Editorial display typography may deliberately depart from the system when its subject and scale justify that role; keep the treatment local rather than letting it leak into routine controls.
