# Motion rhythm

Judge motion over a working session, not only its first playback. A reveal can explain a new relationship once and become a toll on every later visit. Keep repeated operations prompt; retain enough movement to locate a change when instant replacement would lose the object. An expressive introduction can spend more time when the unfolding sequence is itself the content.

Initial response and settling serve different needs. Acknowledgment should arrive promptly; settling gives the eye time to follow the result. Slow initial movement can feel unresponsive even with a short total duration. Choose acceleration, distance and duration together; a small local response and a large spatial transition need not share a timer. An essential action should remain available while decorative motion finishes.

Delay can distinguish accidental contact from intent. A tooltip group may wait on first entry, then respond promptly while the person deliberately explores neighboring controls. Repeating the initial delay for every neighbor discards that evidence of intent; removing it everywhere can make incidental movement noisy.

Replay the ordinary sequence several times at working speed, then inspect a troublesome transition slowly. Look for wasted waiting, competing motion and a recognizable destination. A smoother curve cannot repair an unnecessary transition.
