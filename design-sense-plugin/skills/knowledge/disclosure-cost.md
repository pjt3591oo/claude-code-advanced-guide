# Disclosure cost

Hiding information trades reading effort for finding and remembering it. Keep facts needed to choose, understand a cost or recover from a problem at the decision. Optional explanation can be disclosed when its trigger makes the subject clear. If nearly everyone must open it, reconsider the default composition.

Choose a surface by the work it holds. A short local choice can stay near its trigger; a longer explanation or subtask may need a sheet or page. More space is useful only if the original object, scope and return path remain clear. Contextual help must leave what it explains recognizable and, when needed, operable; fitting inside the viewport is not enough. A mobile viewport does not make every temporary interaction the same kind of sheet.

Comparison needs simultaneous evidence. Sections that can remain open together suit comparing related information; stable switching can suit independent sections used one at a time. Neither helps when a short, well-grouped page already exposes the needed information. Nested disclosure adds another place to lose the task.

When options collapse, keep the chosen value or useful summary visible. Dismissal should not silently commit a draft. Preserve the trigger relationship while the surface is open and retire temporary UI when its owning context disappears.

Test the closed state as seriously as the open one. Can the person predict what is behind the trigger and make the current decision without discovering a hidden prerequisite?
