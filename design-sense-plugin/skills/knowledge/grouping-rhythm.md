# Grouping and rhythm

Read a gap as a relationship. A price closer to the next photograph can appear to belong to the next product. Establish ownership, then make the separation between objects clearer than the separation within one object.

Try spacing and alignment before adding enclosure. A card helps when the group behaves as an independent object; repeated boxes around one continuous task can fragment it. Explicit boundaries may still be needed on complex backgrounds or between independently scrolling panes.

Measure visible content as well as boxes. Matching padding can leave text misaligned because icons contain different internal whitespace. Equal font sizes or icon boxes need not have equal apparent size: glyph proportions, stroke and live area change the visual mass. Correct optical alignment locally while keeping predictable hit areas and repeated structure.

Rhythm does not require identical row heights. Long titles should preserve the relationship between title, metadata and the next item. Compare short, wrapped and missing values together. Temporarily remove a divider or background: retain it if ownership becomes ambiguous, remove it if the relationships become clearer. Equal numerical spacing everywhere rarely expresses unequal relationships well.
